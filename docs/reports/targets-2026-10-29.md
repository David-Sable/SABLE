# Are −14 (Normal) and −19 (Quiet) the right targets? (2026-10-29)

David asked Claude, as co-creator, to test the two targets on the whole library and decide.
**Decision: keep both. Normal −14 LUFS, Quiet −19 LUFS. No change to MATH.md or the app.**

## What was measured
All 418 songs on David's phone (the 392 in `~/dev/sable-corpus/drive` plus the 26 in `phone-extra`; every phone
file was matched to a PC copy by name). Each went through the current engine (`sable_cli --decide`, MATH-1.33).
A song's gain is linear in the target, so one run per song gives the result at any target:
`tools/validation/target_sweep.py` sweeps targets and counts how many songs the true-peak cap (§16.4) stops short
of the target, and by how much. Phone speaker ceiling −1.0 dBTP, Bluetooth −1.5 (§20.1). Quiet includes the
contour's 2.6 dB headroom (§24.4). Albums: the 28 folders with 3+ songs (album loudness approximated by the
duration-weighted power mean of the members' L_I).

Library: median −7.7 LUFS (5–95 %: −12.1 … −4.5), median peak-to-loudness 9.0 dB. So Sable turns most songs down.

The songs short at −14 are the most dynamic masters (peak-to-loudness 11–18 dB): five from *Illmatic*, Breaking
Benjamin's "Dawn", a few JPEGMAFIA tracks, Ice Cube's "The Funeral". They play a little quieter because their peaks
are real. That is artist intent, and Sable does not limit by default.

On the phone (read only, nothing changed): the speaker sits at volume 4/15 and the JBL at 3/15, so the phone has
plenty of volume left. The learning scoreboard's corrections go both ways (JBL 8 up / 5 down, car 3 / 1,
speaker 6 / 1). Nothing shows a target that is clearly too loud or too quiet.

## Why −14 stays
- **Hearing:** about 1 dB is the smallest loudness difference most listeners notice between songs. At −14, 96–97 %
  of songs land within 1 dB of the target, and every album does. Going higher costs fast: −13 nearly doubles the
  misses, −12 triples them.
- **Diminishing return below:** −15 rescues only 6–8 more songs, and it makes everything 1 dB quieter at the same
  knob position. That hurts most on the phone speaker, which is the weakest output.
- **Same level as the rest of the phone:** Spotify (Normal), YouTube and Tidal play at −14, so switching apps
  doesn't jump in volume.
- The perceptual calibration (§12.1, −14 ≈ 75 dB SPL) and the contour design (§24.2) are built around it.

## Why −19 stays
- **Hearing:** 5 dB below Normal sounds roughly 30 % less loud (10 dB is about half as loud). That is clearly
  quieter without sounding like different music, and it is the 75 → 70 phon step the Quiet contour is designed for.
- **Room for the bass lift:** even with the contour's +2.6 dB, 99 % of songs reach the target fully, with at most
  1.8 dB short. −18 would double the misses. −20 only removes 3 small ones (all under 2 dB) and asks for more knob.
- Spotify's Quiet is also −19, so it matches what David is used to.

Reproduce: `python tools/validation/decide_corpus.py` (the 392 drive songs), then
`python tools/validation/target_sweep.py %TEMP%/aud/decide.jsonl` (plus a JSONL for `phone-extra`, made the same way
with a one-off loop on 2026-10-29).
